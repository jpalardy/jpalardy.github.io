## Technical

### Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/0735201811/"><img class="book-cover" src="/assets/best-books-2025/0735201811.jpg" alt="Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques" /></a>

If you are already familiar with stock market charts, then you probably are
very familiar with [candlestick charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlestick_chart). But where do those
come from? And what's the logic behind them?

This book is the answer: this is the book that took an obscure only-in-Japan
concept and [introduced it to the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlestick_chart#History).

Candlestick charts are not very complicated, and a few hours (minutes?) might be enough to understand
how they work. There are certainly [tons of videos](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=candlestick+charts) available to explain it to you, if you want to skip
the book.

Candlestick charts might seem commonplace now, but they were a kind of revolution in _data compression_.
I enjoyed the book, a classic, and the background stories. It's a bit of a dry read, but the summary part
(towards the end) is a good reference.

- book: [amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0735201811/)

## Non-Fiction

### Blood in the Machine

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/0316487740/"><img class="book-cover" src="/assets/best-books-2025/0316487740.jpg" alt="Blood in the Machine" /></a>

This book is centered around the history of the
[Luddites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddite). It also draws parallels with
our contemporary situation regarding big tech and AI.

History has not been kind to the Luddites, [Merriam-Webster defines](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/Luddite) a Luddites as

> one of a group of early 19th century English workmen destroying laborsaving machinery as a protest
>
> broadly: one who is opposed to especially technological change

And though this is _technically_ true, it doesn't tell the whole story.

The Luddites were not ideologically against technology; they were against
**starving to death**, which is what _literally_ happened to some of these
workers and their families.

Imagine learning a trade (in textile) for years, usually under some kind of
indenture, and finally being replaced by a machine, with no viable
alternatives. The Luddites would have been pro-technology _IF_ they
had been the ones owning the machines...

The author has a more comprehensive pitch [here](https://www.stltoday.com/how-to-smash-the-looms-today/article_81bdc24b-fa0f-5128-a36d-d6a19e26069d.html).

- book: [amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0316487740/)
- author: [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Merchant)

## Fiction

### Tai-Pan

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00UWXEBQ4/"><img class="book-cover" src="/assets/best-books-2025/B00UWXEBQ4.jpg" alt="Tai-Pan" /></a>

I [mentioned last year](https://blog.jpalardy.com/posts/best-books-i-read-2024/#sho%CC%84gun) how much
I had loved Shōgun. Now, I have almost finished Clavell's [Asian Saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Saga)
and can recommend it wholeheartedly.

Tai-Pan goes back to pirates, opium smuggling, and the early days of Hong Kong (1840s).
The characters are larger-than-life and we follow the consequences throughout
the rest of the Asian Saga.

There's a definite "Clavell" formula to the books, usually wondering
how people will extricate themselves from (seemingly) impossible situations.
Certainly, it kept me engaged and (relatively) stressed-out.

I only have [Whirlwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlwind_(novel)) left, and I'll be sad when it's over.

- book: [amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00UWXEBQ4/)
- book: [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai-Pan_(novel))
- author: [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clavell)
